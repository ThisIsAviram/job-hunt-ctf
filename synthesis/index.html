Synthesis · November 2026

Who builds the future?

What a year of research on AI, computer science education and work actually shows, organised along three axes. A map of the field, with the questions it raises. Not a position.

This synthesis distils the research behind a strategic discussion held in November 2026 at a national youth technology-education program in Israel: about 190 sorted sources, a research deck in four versions, and an 82-slide talk (in Hebrew, at the bottom of this page). Every number is a snapshot, and forecasts in this field have been wrong, systematically, in both directions.

IN ONE MINUTEThree axes, one picture

Most of what we gathered falls on three axes. They are not separate stories: the speed of the tools drives the change in jobs, and both push education back toward skills that outlast any tool.

AXIS 1

Professions are shifting

The entry door into software work is narrowing for the youngest, while demand moves toward people who can direct, check and own what AI produces. The job title itself is open.

AXIS 2

Pace and radius of change

Tool capability doubles in months, cost falls by orders of magnitude, and the effects reach science, organisations and whole labour markets faster than rules or measurement can follow.

AXIS 3

The return to meaningful skills

Where learning was measured, design beats the model: guided use helps, open use can hurt. In parallel, schools and employers are bringing learning and assessment back into the room.

AXIS 1Professions are shifting

The clearest signal is not that software jobs disappear. It is that the first rung of the ladder changes shape, and that what employers ask for moves from writing code to framing, checking and owning it.

Across these sources, the skills that show up more often are a recognisable set: breaking a problem down and specifying it in writing, judging AI output for correctness and safety, understanding a system end to end, and holding a product decision. “Programmer” describes less and less of the work. Architect, engineer, conductor and builder are all on the table; none has won.

What this does not say. The labour data is descriptive, not causal: interest rates, post-pandemic correction and AI move together. Company figures describe one company. Rising demand in ads is not yet rising demand in hiring.

Open questions

  1. What does a graduate know that a domain expert with AI tools doesn’t, and the other way round?
  2. If the first role demands more, what counts as “ready for a first job” in 2028?
  3. Which skills are best learned on the job, and which must arrive ready from a program?
  4. Is a new job title a rebrand, or a change in what we teach and assess?

AXIS 2Pace and radius of change

The tools change faster than the instruments built to measure them. That speed, more than any single capability, is what makes planning hard: a yearly curriculum cycle meets a capability curve that roughly doubles three times a year.

4.3 moDoubling time of the length of task a model completes alone (7 months over 2019–2024). By May 2026 the leading model handled tasks of at least 16 expert-hours; in 2022 it was about 30 seconds. MeasuredMETR, Time Horizon 1.1, 2026
280×Cheaper for the same capability in about two years. Total spend does not fall: volume grows faster. MeasuredStanford AI Index 2025; Epoch AI
2033–2061Range of expert and forecaster estimates for human-level AI over the last decade, moving in both directions year to year. ContestedAI Impacts; Metaculus
0Pauses that followed the promises to slow down: the 2023 open letter, Bletchley, Seoul, Paris. Parts of the EU AI Act slipped to 2027–2028. ReportedFLI; gov.uk; Sidley Austin

The radius is wide. AI contributed to a Nobel in chemistry (AlphaFold), reached gold at the International Math Olympiad and solved open Erdős problems, while its authors note it is stronger at search and construction than at deep insight. In organisations, eight recent incidents shared five structures: permissions as the blast radius, production faster than review, usage pricing with no ceiling, AI outside the fence, and removing the human layer only to rebuild it later. Historian Yuval Noah Harari put the deeper worry in one image: cows see the farmer but not the market that decides their lives, and we may come to live inside AI-built financial and legal systems we cannot understand.

Context, not cause. The OECD reported in September 2026 that the share of Israelis aged 18–24 not in employment, education or training rose from 17% to 20% between 2015 and 2025, while the OECD average fell from 16% to 13%. The report does not explain why, and nothing ties it to AI. It belongs here as a reminder of who falls between institutions while the ground moves.

What this does not say. Benchmarks measure defined software tasks, not real jobs. Incidents are cases, not failure rates. A metaphor is a lens, not a forecast.

Open questions

  1. If the tool changes several times within one school year, what is stable enough to teach, and what isn’t?
  2. What changes in planning when forecasts themselves are unreliable?
  3. Who is responsible for understanding systems nobody fully built: the user, the organisation, the educator, or no one?
  4. What happens to young people when the first door narrows, and who notices first?

AXIS 3The return to meaningful skills

The education evidence is thinner than the hype, but it points one way: how AI is built into learning matters more than which model is used. And the counter-trend is real: as the machine gets stronger, what is human, physical and verified gains value.

SignalWhat it showsSource
+0.73–1.3 SDLearning gains from an AI tutor built on active-learning principles, Harvard physics, 194 students. MeasuredKestin et al., Sci. Reports, 2025
+0.31 SDSix weeks of teacher-guided AI tutoring in Nigeria. MeasuredWorld Bank WP 11125, 2025
−17%On a test without AI, for high-schoolers who practised with an open chatbot. With a designed tutor: no drop. MeasuredBastani et al., PNAS, 2025
≈0Two years of an AI tutor in middle schools, beyond regular practice. A good tool fails when students don’t use it. MeasuredOreopoulos & Low, NBER, 2026
50 / 67%Comprehension score when developers delegated to AI vs wrote by hand; time savings not significant. ContestedShen & Tamkin, Anthropic, 2026 (52 devs)
58%Of education systems restrict phones in school (about 24% in 2023). Handwritten exam books, in-person assessment tracks and in-person interviews are returning. ReportedUNESCO GEM, 2026; WSJ; Univ. of Sydney

Universities moved first on literacy: Ohio State, Purdue and the Technion now require AI fluency for every student, and none has published outcome data yet. The skills that keep surfacing are older than AI: decomposing problems, evaluating output, understanding systems, domain expertise, and the ability to learn the next tool, which, when tools change every few months, is part of the skill itself.

What this does not say. Most trials are short and small, some are preprints. No published results is not proof of failure. The offline turn is not evidence that offline is better.

Open questions

  1. What must a learner be able to do without AI, and what can safely be left to the tool?
  2. How do we know someone understands, and isn’t just producing: tests, portfolios, defending their code, or a mix?
  3. What do we keep offline on purpose, and can we tell it apart from what is offline by habit?
  4. When the decision comes before the evidence, what makes a program an experiment we can learn from rather than a commitment?

BOUNDARIESWhat this synthesis does not do

It does not recommend a curriculum change, predict when general AI arrives, or attribute causes to trends that were only measured as correlations. It does not replace the discussion it was written for: it is the raw material. Every open question above can get an owner and a way to check it, through data to collect, a small pilot, or a conversation with people already working there. The decision comes after the check, not before.
