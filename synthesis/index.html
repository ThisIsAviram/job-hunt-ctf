Synthesis · November 2026

Who builds the future?

A year of research on AI, work and technology education, organised to support a discussion, not to end one. It maps what is known, shows where Israel’s education system stands, and lays out the questions worth arguing about. It does not answer them.

Research and synthesis by Aviram Weisberg, developed at Be’Netivei Udi (BU) for a strategic discussion on the future of youth technology education in Israel.

IN ONE MINUTEThree axes, one picture

Most of what we gathered falls on three axes. They are not separate stories: the speed of the tools drives the change in jobs, and both push education back toward skills that outlast any tool. Each axis ends in a question, not a conclusion.

AXIS 1

Professions are shifting

The entry door into software work is narrowing for the youngest, while demand moves toward people who can direct, check and own what AI produces.

What does “ready for a first job” mean in 2028?
AXIS 2

Pace and radius of change

Tool capability doubles in months, cost falls by orders of magnitude, and effects spread faster than rules or measurement can follow.

What is stable enough to teach?
AXIS 3

The return to meaningful skills

Where learning was measured, design beats the model. Schools and employers are bringing learning and assessment back into the room.

What must a learner do without AI?

AXIS 1 · PROFESSIONSThe first rung changes shape

The clearest signal is not that software jobs disappear. It is that the first rung of the ladder changes shape, and that what employers ask for moves from writing code to framing, checking and owning it.

SignalWhat it showsSource
−13%Relative employment drop for ages 22–25 in AI-exposed occupations (late 2022 → mid 2025), while older workers in the same jobs held steady or grew.Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen, Stanford, 2025
+14%Software-developer postings year on year, 47% mentioning AI. IT overall is still about 30% below pre-pandemic.Indeed Hiring Lab, May 2026

A view from inside the frontier

An AI company is an unusual employer, but it can work as a leading indicator. In October 2026 Anthropic published an analysis of its own employment data, AI-agent telemetry, hiring records and 17 employee interviews. The pattern matches the first-rung signal above, from the inside.

SignalWhat it showsSource
6 : 1Hours worked by employee-directed AI agents per employee hour by August 2026. Code commits per engineer ran about 5× their level six months earlier, with no measurable rise in reverted code.Anthropic Institute, Oct 2026
10 → 22%Share of employees’ AI sessions spent on “meta-work”: writing specs for agents, reviewing their output, monitoring pipelines. Reading all agent output would take over half the work week even for non-technical staff.Anthropic Institute, Oct 2026
≈ 3×Growth in how often hiring managers mention a candidate’s creative use of AI tools in technical assessments (2024 → 2026). Comments on autonomy and judgment rose most; domain expertise shrank as a share of assessment.Anthropic Institute, Oct 2026

The report also names a tension that belongs in an education discussion: as employees automate their own tasks, some are “increasingly removed from the object-level work that originally built their skills”. Employment protections for incumbents, the authors note, do not help those who are not hired to begin with.

Across these sources, the skills that show up more often are a recognisable set: breaking a problem down and specifying it in writing, judging AI output for correctness and safety, understanding a system end to end, and holding a product decision. “Programmer” describes less and less of the work. Architect, engineer, conductor and builder are all on the table; none has won.

What this does not say. The labour data is descriptive, not causal: interest rates, post-pandemic correction and AI move together. Company figures describe one company. Rising demand in ads is not yet rising demand in hiring.

Open questions

  1. What does a graduate know that a domain expert with AI tools doesn’t, and the other way round?
  2. If the first role demands more, what counts as “ready for a first job” in 2028?
  3. Which skills are best learned on the job, and which must arrive ready from a program?
  4. Is a new job title a rebrand, or a change in what we teach and assess?
  5. If employers weigh judgment over implementation, how do young people build judgment when the implementation work that used to train it is done by agents?

AXIS 2 · PACE AND RADIUSFaster than the instruments

The tools change faster than the instruments built to measure them. That speed, more than any single capability, is what makes planning hard.

SignalWhat it showsSource
16 hLength of expert task the leading model handled alone by May 2026. In 2022 it was about 30 seconds.METR, Time Horizon 1.1, 2026
280×Cheaper for the same capability in about two years. Total spend does not fall: volume grows faster.Stanford AI Index 2025; Epoch AI
2033–2061Range of expert and forecaster estimates for human-level AI over the last decade, moving in both directions year to year.AI Impacts; Metaculus
0Pauses that followed the promises to slow down: the 2023 open letter, Bletchley, Seoul, Paris. Parts of the EU AI Act slipped to 2027–2028.FLI; gov.uk; Sidley Austin

The radius is wide. AI contributed to a Nobel in chemistry (AlphaFold), reached gold at the International Math Olympiad and solved open Erdős problems, while its authors note it is stronger at search and construction than at deep insight. In organisations, eight recent incidents shared five structures: permissions as the blast radius, production faster than review, usage pricing with no ceiling, AI outside the fence, and removing the human layer only to rebuild it later. Historian Yuval Noah Harari put the deeper worry in one image: cows see the farmer but not the market that decides their lives, and we may come to live inside AI-built financial and legal systems we cannot understand.

What this does not say. Benchmarks measure defined software tasks, not real jobs. Incidents are cases, not failure rates. A metaphor is a lens, not a forecast.

Open questions

  1. If the tool changes several times within one school year, what is stable enough to teach, and what isn’t?
  2. What changes in planning when forecasts themselves are unreliable?
  3. Who is responsible for understanding systems nobody fully built: the user, the organisation, the educator, or no one?
  4. What happens to young people when the first door narrows, and who notices first?

AXIS 3 · MEANINGFUL SKILLSDesign decides, not the model

The education evidence is thinner than the hype, but it points one way: how AI is built into learning matters more than which model is used. And as the machine gets stronger, what is human, physical and verified gains value.

SignalWhat it showsSource
50 / 67%Comprehension score when developers delegated to AI vs wrote by hand; time savings not significant.Shen & Tamkin, Anthropic, 2026 (52 devs)
58%Of education systems restrict phones in school (about 24% in 2023). Handwritten exam books, in-person assessment tracks and in-person interviews are returning.UNESCO GEM, 2026; WSJ; Univ. of Sydney
~47%Of university students’ conversations with Claude were “direct”: seeking an answer or a finished output with minimal engagement. Computer-science students were 38.6% of conversations while earning 5.4% of US bachelor’s degrees.Anthropic Education Report, Apr 2025 (~1M conversations)
g ≈ 0.49Average transfer from learning to program to other thinking skills; larger for near transfer than far. Generic “brain training” shows little far transfer.Scherer et al., 2019; Sala & Gobet, 2017
4 domainsThe new OECD–EC framework splits AI literacy into Engage, Create, Manage and Shape AI. It will underpin the first PISA test of media and AI literacy in 2029.OECD & European Commission, June 2026

Universities moved first on literacy: Ohio State, Purdue and the Technion now require AI fluency for every student, and none has published outcome data yet. The skills that keep surfacing are older than AI: decomposing problems, evaluating output, understanding systems, domain expertise, and the ability to learn the next tool, which, when tools change every few months, is part of the skill itself.

What this does not say. Most trials are short and small, some are preprints. No published results is not proof of failure. The offline turn is not evidence that offline is better.

Open questions

  1. What must a learner be able to do without AI, and what can safely be left to the tool?
  2. How do we know someone understands, and isn’t just producing: tests, portfolios, defending their code, or a mix?
  3. What do we keep offline on purpose, and can we tell it apart from what is offline by habit?
  4. When the decision comes before the evidence, what makes a program an experiment we can learn from rather than a commitment?

THE ISRAELI LENSWhat the system has decided, and what it hasn’t

Israel is not waiting. In 2026 the Ministry of Education set targets for AI competence across the school system and opened a selective AI major for high school. The decisions are real. What is still open is who each one is for, who will teach it, and how anyone will know it worked.

What has been decided

DecisionWhat it saysSource
80%Target share of pupils from grade 4 up with basic AI competence in 2026–27 (“AI for all”). Alongside it: 60% of grade 10–12 students experiencing AI in a matriculation exam, and 70% of teaching staff with basic competence.Ministry of Education, Strategic focus for 2026–27 (Apr 2026)
AI1–3Middle-school computer science: three AI units woven into the existing Python A–C courses, replacing the old track fully by 2028–29. Exam questions involving language models already appear in Python B (grade 8) and C (grade 9).CS chief-inspector circular (Aug 2026)

50 → 300Teachers planned for the major: about 50 in year one, 200–300 within four years. Partners named include the Weizmann Institute, the Cyber Education Center and Google.pc.co.il interview with the Ministry (Aug 2026)

What the market around it shows

The Innovation Authority found three times as many companies saying they cut hiring because of AI adoption, while the same firms still hired 8% and laid off 2.8%: gradual adjustment, not a shock. Placement firms describe a market “not open to juniors”. That is the same first-rung pattern seen in the US data, at Israeli scale.

DISCUSSION WINDOWSWhat the room argued about

The discussion was built around windows: short pauses where a tension was put on the table with two positions held on purpose. They are reproduced here as they were posed, without the room’s conclusions.

WINDOW 1 EXPERTISE

The teacher who built an app

A maths teacher builds an adaptive practice app for her class over a weekend, without writing code. What does someone who studied technology know that she doesn’t, and what does she know that they don’t? Who is responsible when pupils’ data leaks?

WINDOW 2 EQUITY

When the bar rises

Beyond “programmer”, who are we prepared to leave behind? And if the graduate is no longer a programmer, what is the name of the person we are preparing: architect, engineer, conductor, builder, or a role that doesn’t exist yet?

WINDOW 3 TIMING

Lead, or wait for the employer?

Everything is moving and the main destination of graduates has not changed what it asks for. Change programs before being asked, or hold the bar accepted today? If AI props weaker learners up, will they feel successful without understanding the logic?

WINDOW 4 FOUNDATIONS

What is worth teaching?

Code without AI until thinking is built, or AI from day one, learning to think through the tool? What must a learner be able to do without it, and who updates the volatile layer, at what pace?

OPEN THREAD ASSESSMENT

Understanding or producing?

How do we know a learner understands and isn’t just producing? Exams, portfolios, defending one’s own code in person, or a mix, and which of these survives at scale?

OPEN THREAD TEACHERS

Who teaches in 2028?

The system plans tens of teachers for a new major while tools change several times in one school year. How are educators prepared, and is the educator now part of the product?

THREE FRAMINGSWhat exactly are we teaching?

Under every window sits a more basic choice. “AI education” usually means one of three different things, each with its own logic, evidence, cost and shelf life. Naming them separately makes the discussion sharper. This page does not choose between them.

FRAMING A

Teach working with AI

AI as a way of working: using, prompting, delegating, checking and owning what a tool produces, in any subject.

Can every learner use AI well, and know when not to?

In Israel now
“AI for all”: the 80% competence target, AI in matriculation exams, LLM questions in middle-school CS exams.
Elsewhere
OECD–EC Engage, Create and Manage domains; AI-fluency requirements at Ohio State, Purdue and the Technion; Estonia’s school-wide AI Leap.
What the evidence says
Designed use helps; open use can hurt the test without AI. How it is built in decides the effect.
The question it leaves open
How do we avoid teaching an interface that expires, and output without understanding?
FRAMING B

Teach the science of AI

AI as a discipline: how models learn, the maths underneath, data, evaluation, building and shaping systems.

Who will understand, build and question these systems from the inside?

In Israel now
The selective 15-unit AI scientists major and its grade-9 preparatory course, about 50 schools each.
Elsewhere
UNESCO’s “AI techniques” and “system design”; the OECD–EC “Shape AI” domain; at least 8 hours of AI a year in Beijing schools from autumn 2025.
What the evidence says
Durable concepts outlast tools, but there are no outcome studies yet for school-level AI majors.
The question it leaves open
Who gets in when selection runs on maths scores, and where do the teachers come from?
FRAMING C

Teach the capacity to adapt

The skills to cope with change at this pace: decomposing problems, judging output, understanding systems, learning the next tool, persisting.

What will still matter after the next three tools?

In Israel now
Present in rhetoric and in some programs, not yet as a measured, assessed strand of the curriculum.
Elsewhere
WEF: 39% of core skills expected to change by 2030; UNESCO’s “human-centred mindset”.
What the evidence says
Mixed. Far transfer from generic training is weak; learning to program transfers moderately (g ≈ 0.49).
The question it leaves open
Can it be taught and assessed on its own, or only through a real domain?

One way to read the three framings is by which layer each feeds. A syllabus updates about once a year; the tools above it move about seven times as far in that time (Figure 2).

The questions that decide the mix

Every real program mixes all three. The disagreement is about proportions and order, and it turns on questions this page leaves open:

  1. For whom? A selective pipeline into technical roles and a universal program for every teenager may need opposite mixes.
  2. In what order? Foundations before the tool, the tool as the way into foundations, or both at once with deliberate “no AI” zones?
  3. Measured how? If framing C cannot be assessed, will it survive the first budget review? If A is assessed only with AI switched on, will anyone notice what is lost?
What this does not say. The three framings are a lens for discussion, not a taxonomy from the literature, and the evidence behind each is uneven. The Israeli decisions are new; none has outcome data yet.

RUN THE CONVERSATIONFrom a question to a check

This page is raw material for a discussion, not a substitute for one. The structure that worked in the room is simple enough to reuse.

STEP 1 · FEEL IT

Start with the game

Twelve minutes on the job board give everyone the same concrete reference before anyone argues about the future.

STEP 2 · PICK A WINDOW

Choose one tension

Take one discussion window or one framing. Hold both positions on purpose before anyone takes a side.

STEP 3 · OWN THE CHECK

Give it an owner

Every open question gets an owner and a way to check it: data to collect, a small pilot, or a conversation with people already doing it. The decision comes after the check.

What this synthesis does not do. It does not recommend a curriculum change, predict when general AI arrives, or attribute causes to trends that were only measured as correlations.

ABOUT THIS WORKWhere it was made

This research and synthesis were carried out at Be’Netivei Udi (BU), an Israeli nonprofit running national technology-education programs for teenagers, as preparation for an internal strategic discussion held in November 2026.

The views and framings are the author’s and do not represent an official organisational position. Public data is cited as published; internal program details were left out.
